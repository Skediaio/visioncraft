# Vegetation: sources, licences and how plants get into VisionCraft

Where plants, leaves and bark come from, what may be shipped with the library, what a user may
load for themselves, and what is planned. Written 2026-11-14. Claims about third-party licences
change over time: each one below says how it was checked. Check the source's own licence page
before relying on it.

## The rule

The library that ships with VisionCraft accepts **CC0 and CC-BY 4.0 only**, with licence, author
and source recorded per asset (`AGENTS.md` rules 6 and 7). `visioncraft-assets check` refuses
anything else and regenerates `assets/ATTRIBUTION.md`.

Three different things are easy to confuse:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| May it be shipped in the VisionCraft library? | Only CC0 or CC-BY 4.0. |
| May a user load it into their own project? | Yes, if their licence for it allows the use. It stays in their project; we do not redistribute it. |
| May its file format or content be taken from a closed visualisation package? | No (rule 6), whatever the price. |

"Free to download" is not "free to redistribute". Most free offers below fail the first question
and pass the second.

## What the library has today

| Kind | Count | Source | Licence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Models (plants, furniture, lamps, props) | 311 | Poly Haven | CC0 |
| Materials at 1k, with 2k and 4k tiers | 115 | ambientCG, Poly Haven | CC0 |
| Skies at 1k, with a 4k tier | 12 | Poly Haven | CC0 |
| Procedural species (`visioncraft-flora`) | 118 | generated by our own code | MIT (code); output is ours |
| Scanned leaf atlases with per-leaf cut-outs | 29 | ambientCG | CC0 |
| Scanned bark sets | 19 | ambientCG, Poly Haven | CC0 |

Indexes are tracked in git (`assets/catalog.json`, `materials.json`, `skies.json`, `tiers.json`,
`flora.json`); the files themselves are not. `tools/visioncraft-assets` fetches and ingests them.

Scanned leaf coverage of the 118 species: 0 exact species, 16 same genus (3 by the source's own
tags, 13 identified by eye and not botanically verified), 84 by leaf shape class (a look-alike,
not the plant), 18 none (conifers, palms, ginkgo, peony, lupin, sedum, creeper, water lily,
tomato, and cranesbill: its sheet's rounded, shallowly lobed leaves made 'Rozanne' a ball of
discs). Those 18 keep drawn leaves. Hedge forms (a clipped hornbeam) take their genus' leaf
shapes in their own drawn colour. LeafSet014 (hornbeam): three of its six cut-outs had the tip
taken for the petiole and were turned round by hand in `flora.json`; the asset tool's petiole
finder still mistakes a long drip tip for a petiole and would find them again on a fresh ingest.

| [Quixel Megascans on Fab](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=179945) | Fab Standard License: use in your own projects and renders, no redistribution of the assets themselves | Yes, in their own project, under Fab's terms. | Xfrog models | Reported as CC BY-NC-ND on Sketchfab (non-commercial, no derivatives). Not verified here | For non-commercial use only, if that report is right |
| Maxtree, Evermotion, Botaniq, Graswald, CGAxis and similar | Paid, royalty-free for use, no redistribution | Yes, bought and loaded by the user |
| Share Textures | Its licence forbids redistribution (already excluded in the asset tool) | Yes |
| AI-generated models and textures | No clear author or provenance; text- and image-to-3D also handles thin foliage badly | Owner's decision; not in the library by default |

Paid sets are the only place where the same plant comes as a series of growth stages (for example
a European ash in 24 models, or an aspen in five stages, on CGTrader). None was found under a
free licence.

## Generators

Growth over the years needs a plant whose form changes with age, which a downloaded model cannot
do. That is why the main route is generating, not downloading.

| Generator | Licence | Notes | Checked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `visioncraft-flora` (ours) | MIT | 118 species, seasons, three LODs, age changes the form, impostors; runs in the browser | Our code |
| [EZ-Tree](https://github.com/dgreenheck/ez-tree) | MIT (package registry); bundled textures carry their own licences | JavaScript on three.js; seed and parameters in, tree out; LOD generation; GLB export in its web app | Search result; the library's own export call and texture licences not read |
| [Modular Tree](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/modular-tree/) | Core MIT, Blender add-on GPL-3 | Growth in iterations with apical dominance | Search result |
| Blender Sapling Tree Gen | GPL (part of Blender) | The output of a GPL tool is the user's own | From memory |
| EcoMod | GPL | Age and environment parameters; GPL code cannot go into this MIT project | Search result |

Using another generator has two possible forms, with different consequences:

- **As an offline step in the asset tool** (run it, save GLBs with LODs into the library): quick
  variety, but the result is a static model. It only scales with age and season; it does not grow,
  drop its leaves per species, or take scanned leaves from `flora.json`.
- **As ideas or MIT code ported into `visioncraft-flora`** (a better branch solver, for example):
  more work, but every species keeps growth, seasons, LODs and the browser path.

No decision has been taken between these. The second fits the product's direction better; the
first is the faster way to more silhouettes.

## How a user adds their own plants

- **A model they own:** import a `.glb` in the app (file picker or drop). It becomes a project
  object `user:<id>`, usable with every planting tool, saved inside `.visioncraft.zip`. Commands:
  `object.add`, `object.rename`, `object.remove`. It scales along the growth curve; its form does
  not change.
- **A species of their own:** the species editor changes the generator's parameters and saves a
  custom species, which then grows and follows the seasons like the built-in ones.
- **Not yet:** a user species made of several models as growth stages (age to model, scaled in
  between). Planned as an extension for people who own such series.

## What holds realism back, in order

1. Leaves: drawn leaf textures read as plastic. Scanned leaf cut-outs on the generated skeleton
   are being wired in; more atlases unlock more species.
2. Bark: 19 sets, 8 matched by genus.
3. Tree form: generated; a better branch solver would give more believable silhouettes.
4. Variety: one form per species reads as a catalogue, several variants per species read as a
   garden. The generator has variants by seed; planting tools should use more of them.
5. The ground: no fallen leaves, stones or soft lawn edges yet.

## Plan

In this order; items 1 to 3 are in progress.

1. Scanned leaves and bark in the generator, with a per-species Scanned/Drawn switch and a note
   when a set is a look-alike.
2. The remaining ambientCG leaf sets and Poly Haven leaf textures; report coverage again.
3. Ground clutter as a generator (fallen leaves by species and season, stones, lawn edges).
4. Growth data: the table is estimated. Check it against open data (Wikidata, GBIF, Trefle) and
   mark each value's source.
5. Reference scans: a candidate list of CC0 botanical scans (ffish.asia / floraZia, Smithsonian)
   for the species we have, to compare generated forms against real plants, and a few reduced
   hero plants where a scan is clearly better than the generator.
6. Tree form: decide between porting a branch solver into `visioncraft-flora` and generating
   static trees offline (see Generators).
7. User species with several models as growth stages.
